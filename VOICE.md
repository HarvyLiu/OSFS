# VOICE.md — how this book sounds

Every lesson is a **chapter in a book**, written by an author teaching a student
sitting next to them. The contract machinery (frontmatter, sections, codeblocks,
line tables, `Change X → Y`, quizzes, figures) is scaffolding — the voice wraps
it, never removes it.

## The narrator

- Second person, warm authority. "You" do things; "we" notice things together.
- An author, not a manual. Opens with a scene, a question, or a small story —
  never with a definition.
- Confident about what's load-bearing and what's trivia. Say which is which.

## Rhythm of a chapter

1. **Hook** — one vivid line (the `>` quote). A claim or image, not a summary.
2. **Scene** — 2–4 sentences placing the reader somewhere real (a dump, a
   crash, a machine room, 1981). Then the idea, plainly.
3. **Hands** — the existing Simulate/Build/Use beats, unchanged in substance.
   Verified commands, tables, and outputs are **never reworded into
   wrongness**; voice edits touch connective prose only.
4. **Closer** — one line saying what the reader now owns and where it returns
   (forward pointer to a later chapter).

## Rules

- Short sentences. Contractions welcome. No hype adjectives, no emojis.
- Banned: delve, tapestry, landscape (as metaphor), seamless, robust (as praise),
  "in today's fast-paced world", "it's important to note".
- Metaphors must pay rent: every analogy earns its keep within two sentences
  or it gets cut.
- Name the trick: "this is the whole trick", "the one-bit revolution",
  "claims need evidence". Beginners remember sentences, not paragraphs.
- Honest labels over daunting ones: say when something is a simplification,
  a preview, or "enough for now".
- History in homeopathic doses: one date/name per chapter max, only if it
  explains *why* (A20, `0xAA55`, `$` before immediates).
- Jargon arrives defined-then-used, with a glossary link on first use —
  the rule from AGENTS.md, enforced with authorial grace, not footnotes.

## What never changes for voice

Frontmatter keys, section names/order, fenced code contents, commands,
expected outputs, quiz questions, figure files. Voice lives in the prose
between them.
